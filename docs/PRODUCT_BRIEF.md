# FOLILUNE — Product Brief
_Status: v0.1, preproduction, 11 November 2026. This is a working brief, not final feature approval._

## The vision
Create a premium, personal reading environment for tablets and laptops that supports many forms of writing and visual storytelling. It should feel effortless on day one, then reveal depth through exploration, context and personal choice.

**North star:** A person can begin reading within moments, remain immersed without interface friction, and discover useful powers only when relevant.

## Confirmed direction (from product owner)
- Tablet and laptop experiences are priorities.
- Exceptional interaction and visual quality are essential.
- Advanced capabilities should be present without cluttering the first experience.
- Reveal features gradually; do not dump functionality onto initial screens.
- Support novels, comics, and a broad range of books and written formats.
- Treat this as a standalone reading product.

## Preliminary reader groups to validate
1. Immersive fiction readers who value atmosphere and uninterrupted flow.
2. Visual-story readers (comics, manga, graphic novels) needing art-respecting controls.
3. Students and nonfiction readers who annotate, cross-reference, and revisit ideas.
4. Readers who switch between devices, accessibility modes, or listening and reading.
These are hypotheses, not yet validated market segments.

## Proposed experience pillars (not locked features)
- **Read:** formats adapted to their content, typographic craftsmanship, comfort.
- **Collect:** a beautiful, organized, searchable personal library.
- **Understand:** contextual dictionary, translation, notes, character maps, summaries and optional AI, controlled by the reader.
- **Reflect:** bookmarks, highlights, reading journal, knowledge connections, thoughtful progress.
- **Continue:** offline-first reading and reliable device continuity when possible.

## Core user journeys to prototype
1. First launch → import or choose a legally available sample → begin reading with minimal setup.
2. Reopen → immediately resume at last meaningful position.
3. Reading prose → select a passage → get lightweight definitions, notes or deeper tools if requested.
4. Reading manga/comics → choose flow/orientation → navigate without damaging art or revealing spoilers.
5. Study session → highlight text, open contextual notes side-by-side, export reader-owned annotations.
6. Build a collection → organize works and rediscover something worth reading.

## Product quality requirements
- Content always has visual priority over effects, controls, ads and AI.
- Layouts must be genuinely designed for large displays and resizing.
- Accessibility by design: scalable type, contrast, screen-reader controls, logical focus, reduced motion, keyboard navigation and localization readiness.
- Transparent data handling, portable library/annotations and cautious privacy defaults.
- Robustness across big files, low memory, interrupted import and offline use.
- Performance measures and targets to be specified before development, not guessed now.

## Explicit exclusions for now
- No commercial copyrighted book or comic catalog without rights.
- No promise that DRM-protected files from third-party stores can be imported.
- No requirement for social network, marketplace, subscription or generative features in the first release.
- No automated AI analysis of private content by default.
- No fixed technology stack, visual mockup, release date, or pricing.

## Discovery questions
- Which device and OS combinations matter first (Android tablets, Windows, web, iPad, macOS)?
- Which three reading formats represent the most important first-use cases?
- Is FOLILUNE primarily a reader for user-provided materials, a discovery platform, or both?
- Should audiobook playback, read-along and reading assistance ship in the initial release?
- How much offline use and synchronization are needed, under what budget?
- Which interactions make reading genuinely better rather than adding novelty?

## Initial validation criteria
- A new reader can find/import a work and begin with little confusion.
- The reading surface stays focused while features remain discoverable.
- Readers can understand the controls on a tablet and a laptop.
- The chosen file formats render accurately, keep progress and preserve content rights.
- A usability test reveals no essential function hidden beyond reasonable discovery.
